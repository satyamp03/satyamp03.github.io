02 · Research

What I'm working on

Most of what I do comes back to one theme: extracting meaningful signal from noisy, stochastic, high-dimensional data, and asking whether the models we build actually understand the mechanism underneath.

Evaluation

GAME: Genomic API for Model Evaluation

There are a lot of deep learning models for genomic sequence now, and comparing them fairly is harder than it sounds. Each one expects its own inputs, runs in its own software environment, and usually gets tested on its own favourite benchmarks. GAME fixes that by putting models and benchmarks behind a shared API, so any model can be run against any benchmark, wherever each of them happens to live. I'm co-first author on the preprint.

  • I wrote the orchestrator that splits large prediction jobs across HPC nodes and stitches the results back together (FastAPI, asyncio).
  • I also built a matcher that uses a local LLM (Gemma 3 through Ollama) to line up messy task, cell-type and species labels between models and benchmarks.

What's next

Working on models that understand the regulatory mechanism

A model can score well on a benchmark by picking up quirks of the reference genome or the assay instead of real regulatory logic, and the score alone won't tell you which. That's what I want to dig into next: models for sparse and single-nucleotide-resolution data that are interpretable from the start, not explained after the fact. I'm still working out where interpretability belongs best: in the architecture, in the training objective, or in a layer on top.

I've also started playing with diffusion and flow matching for generating sequences. It's early days, but it's what I'm most excited about right now.
