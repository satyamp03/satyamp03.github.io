01 · Research

From evaluating models to generating sequence

One thread runs through my work: before you trust a genomic model, you need to know what it actually learned. That question moves me from evaluation, through representation, toward generation.

Evaluation

GAME: Genomic API for Model Evaluation

Co-first author of an open-source framework that decouples genomic deep-learning models (Predictors) from benchmark datasets (Evaluators) behind REST APIs, so sequence-to-function models can be compared reproducibly across independent compute environments.

  • Scatter–gather load orchestrator (FastAPI, asyncio) that spreads large batches across HPC nodes and reassembles predictions
  • LLM-powered matcher (Gemma 3 via Ollama) that reconciles free-text task, cell-type and species labels between models and benchmarks
  • Containerised AlphaGenome, Borzoi, ChromBPNet and DREAM-RNN with Apptainer/Conda on SLURM clusters
Code on GitHub →
Representation

Did the model learn mechanism or a shortcut?

Genomic models can quietly latch onto reference-genome or assay artifacts rather than regulatory logic. I am building representation-learning architectures for single-nucleotide-resolution and sparse genomic data, with interpretability as a design goal rather than an afterthought.

  • Where should interpretability live: architecture, training objective, or a post-hoc explanation layer?
  • How do we test for mechanism, not just accuracy?
Generation

Diffusion and flow matching for sequence

In progress: diffusion and flow-matching approaches for single-nucleotide-resolution genomic modelling. The same family of methods now drives protein design, which is why I see regulatory DNA design and protein engineering as one problem on two substrates.

  • Evaluation informs what a good generative model must get right
  • Longer term: generative design of regulatory sequence

02 · Path

From black holes and dark photons, to DNA and ML

I started in physics and astronomy. Reconstructing rare signals from enormous simulated event streams turned out to be good training for asking what a genomic model is really seeing.

  1. Oct 2024 – present

    Research Assistant & Lab Technician · de Boer Lab

    School of Biomedical Engineering, UBC

    Built GAME and its orchestration and matching modules, ran large GPU benchmarking workloads on UBC ARC Sockeye and Digital Research Alliance of Canada clusters, and now work on generative sequence modelling.

  2. Aug 2023 – Aug 2024

    Research Assistant & Lab Technician · Adams Lab

    Beaty Biodiversity Research Centre, UBC

    End-to-end bulk and single-cell RNA-seq pipelines (STAR, HISAT2, DESeq2, edgeR) and alternative-splicing analysis with rMATS and SUPPA2, in polyploid Brassica napus.

  3. Sep 2022 – Apr 2023

    Honours thesis · Search for low-mass dark photons at Belle II

    Belle II group, UBC · supervised by Dr. Christopher Hearty

    Analysed over 20 million simulated Monte Carlo events with tracking and clustering algorithms, and designed signal-selection cuts for an e⁺e⁻γ signature, validated against Bhabha and digamma backgrounds across four run conditions.

  4. Feb 2020 – May 2023

    Undergraduate Research Assistant · Quantum Materials Design Lab

    Stewart Blusson Quantum Matter Institute · Dr. Meigan Aronson & Dr. Alannah Hallas

    Crystal growth and characterisation of topological materials (neutron scattering, XRD, SEM/STM, PPMS/MPMS), Rietveld refinements, and eight years of growth data organised with Pandas and SQL. Co-authored work on spin dynamics in a metallic spin chain.

  5. 2018 – 2023

    BSc, Combined Honours in Physics and Astronomy

    University of British Columbia

    Outside the lab: advisor and outreach coordinator for the UBC Astronomy Club, and TA for introductory physics supporting 2000+ students.
